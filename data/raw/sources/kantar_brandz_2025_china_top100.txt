Welcome




          Moving for ward

          At Kantar, building strong brands is, quite literally,
          our mission. We shape the brands of the future
          by better understanding people everywhere.
          We use the power of our teams, tools, and data to
          become the partners clients need to implement
          transformational change.

          And for 20 years, the Kantar BrandZ database has
          been one of the most important tools in Kantar’s
          brand-building arsenal. At the core of the Kantar
          BrandZ approach is a model for stable growth that
          has been tested and re-tested, challenged and
          re-confirmed, across all the work that we do.

          That now includes the Blueprint for Brand Growth,
          Kantar’s breakthrough strategic framework for
          understanding how businesses can build profitable,
          strong, and sustainable brands. Drawing on Kantar
          BrandZ and marketplace data from around the
          world, the Blueprint for Brand Growth presents a
          trove of best practices that truly cross borders. It
          should prove an essential companion for Chinese
          brands navigating through this challenging
          economic environment.

          As mentioned, we began our Kantar BrandZ Global
          rankings back in 2006. Over the years, Kantar’s data
          and modelling has indicated how brand value – and
          branded business growth – derives from three core
          building blocks: Meaningfulness, Difference, and
          Salience. Brand attributes like Creativity, Innovation,
          Sustainability, and Trust (all of which are measured
          in the Kantar BrandZ database) ultimately
          contribute to a brand’s Meaningful Difference.

          Today, our proprietary Kantar BrandZ database
          has expanded to include information from over
          4.5 million consumers regarding their attitudes
          toward (and relationships with) 22,000 brands
          across 538 categories in 54 markets. All of that
          has produced more than 5.5 billion data points.

          Just two years into our project, in 2008, the global
          economy ground to a halt, and brands were tested
          in unprecedented ways. So, too, was Kantar’s theory
          of brand value growth. But the data didn’t lie: when
          the dust settled, it was clear that Meaningfully
          Different brands had retained more of their value
          during the initial period of the market crash, and
          captured more of the gains from the ensuing
          recovery once the economy got back on track.
          Year after year, this data has told a consistent story:
          in good times and bad, it pays to bet on strong,
          distinctive brands.

Overview




Taken together, the rebates have helped unlock pent-
up demand – particularly around major sales events
like the China 618 Shopping Festival. That’s no small
feat in a country with a famously high savings rate
(which climbed even higher after the pandemic).
However, some localities’ ‘trade-in’ programmes
are starting to max out on their funding; long-term
momentum will ultimately require further innovation
and economic transformation.

China’s tech titans, meanwhile, have rebounded
strongly after years of regulatory uncertainty.
AI breakthroughs like DeepSeek-R1 and Qwen3
(Alibaba) have fuelled excitement in China’s wider
tech ecosystem, with listed public companies
enjoying strong stock rallies. On the hardware side,
domestic chipmakers have come into their own
in the face of import controls.

In order to meet ambitious growth targets, China’s
manufacturers have accelerated their expansions
abroad – especially into friendlier markets across
ASEAN, the Middle East, and Latin America. Overall,
a weaker yuan has helped offset some of the
volume declines caused by worldwide economic
malaise. And so, too, has the emergence of Chinese
brands as trusted names worth paying more for –
which, of course, is precisely what makes branding
so valuable both at home and abroad.

While top brands can never be fully immune to market
swoons or economic shocks, strong brands are better
positioned to grow beyond any limitations set by
category, geography, or macroeconomic context.

E XPERIENTIAL
RE TAIL
Chinese malls are changing. First, pandemic
vacancies opened the door for new types of
tenants. Now, anchor spaces that once went to
luxury beauty brands are being reimagined with
a diverse array of experiential formats. You’ll find
Pop Mart toy stores, NEV showrooms, upscale
tea bars like CHAGEE, and outdoor gear hubs
like Arc’teryx. The logic is simple: young shoppers
want what they can’t get online. That means
experiences, expertise, and physical interaction.
For brands, the mall is no longer just a billboard
or a sales clearinghouse – it’s a lab for new kinds
of hands-on storytelling.

In the past, reputational concerns sometimes
scared brands away from social media: Would they
lose control of the message? Would these platforms
really be brand-safe? And would consumers really
want to hear from official brand accounts? Or was
KOL-led performance marketing still the only social
media format that could work?

Today, the path forward is clear: consumer attitudes
have shifted to the point where it now makes sense
or brands to embrace a wide variety of social
media marketing formats. For instance, the Kantar
Media Reactions 2024 report shows that consumers
increasingly trust – and are thus open to being
influenced by – content from official brand accounts.

Ultimately, and as our three examples show,
brands have much to look forward to now that the
performance marketing bubble has deflated. They
can use their social media accounts in impactful
new ways, launching new products and promotional
videos via direct interaction with consumers. In the
process, brands can more clearly communicate the
value they create for consumers and build stronger
relationships with their audience – while still
boosting sales in the marketplace.
